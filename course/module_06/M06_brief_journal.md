# Technical Journal: sixth entry

**Where:** your `sec-480` repository, at `journal/module-06.md`
**Due:** Thursday 15 October, 9:00 pm, with the rest of the module.
**Length:** short. A few paragraphs, not an essay.

## What is being graded

Not whether you were right. Whether there is an entry, and whether each answer engages with the
question that was actually asked.

An answer that is two words, or the question pasted back, or an answer to a different question,
gets partial credit. A missing heading gets none. An honest answer saying a thing did not work
and why gets full credit.

That is the whole standard. It is deliberately cheap to satisfy and impossible to fake.

## This module's entry

Six headings, a few sentences each. **An act is one of the numbered sections of the lab**, and
the labels below point back at those sections in `M06_lab_directing-the-work.md`.

1. **My repository, seen as a stranger would see it.** Act 1. What did you find that made sense
   only to you? Write what you expected to find and what was actually there.

2. **The plan that turned out to be wrong.** Act 1. Something in it was. Which part, and what did
   you learn from it being wrong that you would not have learned from it being right?

3. **Criteria written before any output existed.** Act 2. Was that harder than it sounds? What
   did you find you had never actually decided until you had to write it down?

4. **The intervention I nearly did not make.** Act 3. A moment you were unsure whether to step in.
   What made you step in, or not, and would you do the same again?

5. **What a log with no interventions in it would mean.** Act 3. Be honest about your own. If
   yours is short, say whether that is because the work went well or because you stopped
   watching. Both are real answers and only one of them is comfortable.

6. **The criterion that was wrong.** Act 4. One of the things you asked for turned out not to be
   the thing that mattered. Which, and what would you ask for instead? If you think all of them
   held, say what would have had to happen for one of them to fail.

## What a good entry is not

Not a summary of the lab. Not a list of commands. Not a status report saying the work is
complete.

The test is whether someone reading it learns something they could not get from your files.
