# Showing your work

**SEC 480: Advanced AI Practicum** | Reference, good for the whole term

This course grades your process alongside your product. That sentence is easy to nod at and
hard to act on, so this is the practical version: what to save, where it goes, and what
separates evidence from noise.

## Why, before how

You do not cite AI use in this course. It is assumed on every piece of work, and a line saying
so on every file would tell a reader nothing they did not already know.

What is not assumed is how you got there. That part has to be visible, for two reasons.

**Reproducibility.** Six weeks from now you will need to reproduce a result. The prompts and
the steps are the method. A result whose method is undocumented is not reproducible, and an
irreproducible result is not evidence, whether or not a machine was involved.

**Judgment is the thing being taught.** A correct answer tells me you got a correct answer.
The path to it tells me whether you knew why it was correct, what you tried first, and where
you checked instead of trusting. That is the actual subject of this course.

## What to save

Alongside the deliverable, in the same repository:

| Save | Why |
|---|---|
| The prompts you actually used | The method. Paste them as they were, not cleaned up |
| The working session, exported or copied | The full record, including the parts that went sideways |
| What you verified yourself, and how | The difference between a result and a claim |
| What you tried and abandoned | Cheap to write, and it is often the most interesting part |

You do not need all four on every small exercise. Prompts and verification are the floor.

## The habit worth building

Separate what the tool produced from what you confirmed.

> I asked why the integration would not read my repository, and the answer suggested the
> authorization was scoped to public repositories only. I checked the setting myself in GitHub
> before changing anything, and that was in fact what it was.

Two sentences. The first says where an idea came from, the second says how it was checked.
That separation is what makes a record useful rather than decorative, and it is the habit that
transfers to every technical job you will hold.

## The failure mode

The common mistake is recording that a tool was used without recording what was asked.

"I used AI for this assignment" satisfies nothing. It does not tell a reader what came from
where, it does not let you reproduce anything, and it reads as a formality rather than a
method.

The prompts carry the information. Save them.

## Where this shows up

- **Every weekly exercise.** Prompts and verification saved with the work.
- **Your technical journal.** The reflective layer on top of this: what worked, what did not,
  what you would do differently.
- **Unit projects and the final project.** Same expectation, larger scale, and the process
  record is part of what is graded rather than an attachment to it.

A correct answer with no evidence of how you reached it will not receive full credit. A wrong
answer with clear, well-reasoned working will not lose all of it. That is not a slogan, it is
how the rubric actually runs.
