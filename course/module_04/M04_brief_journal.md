# Technical Journal: fourth entry

**Where:** your `sec-480` repository, at `journal/module-04.md`
**Due:** Thursday 1 October, 9:00 pm, with the rest of the module.
**Length:** short. A few paragraphs, not an essay.
**Worth:** 6 points.

## What is being graded

Not whether you were right. Whether there is an entry, and whether each answer engages with the
question that was actually asked.

An answer that is two words, or the question pasted back, or an answer to a different question,
gets partial credit. A missing heading gets none. An honest answer saying a thing did not work
and why gets full credit.

That is the whole standard. It is deliberately cheap to satisfy and impossible to fake.

## This module's entry

Six headings, a few sentences each. **An act is one of the numbered sections of the lab**, and the
labels below point back at those sections in `M04_lab_leaving-the-room.md`.

1. **Planning first, and what I could not see.** Act 1. You set up the four issues before any
   work. Did that change how you went about the acts? And what could you not see about a piece of
   work from where you were standing, before it ran, while it ran, and after?

2. **The Project read-back.** Act 2. What came back changed, or what survived intact, and what
   you make of a settings screen that showed everything in place either way.

3. **Comparing two models, and choosing one.** Act 3. What differed between them, if anything
   did, and what you gave up by picking the one you picked. If they came out the same, say so.
   That is a real answer about this work and it is more useful than a manufactured distinction.

4. **The permissions wording, and the fact that there is more than one of it.** Act 3. The
   product describes the same setting in more than one place and the descriptions disagree. Which
   one were you relying on while your task ran, and does it change how you feel about having left
   it running?

5. **The thing I could not tell from the result.** Act 3. You were not watching. Name one way
   it could have gone wrong without the output looking any different, and say how you would
   find out.

6. **Where the failure moved.** The unit's claim is that doing the work you get it wrong, and
   directing it you fail to notice it went wrong. Did that hold this week, or does it read as a
   neat line that did not match what you experienced? Disagreeing is a valid answer if you say
   what you saw.

## What a good entry is not

Not a summary of the lab. Not a list of commands. Not a status report saying the work is
complete.

The test is whether someone reading it learns something they could not get from your files.
