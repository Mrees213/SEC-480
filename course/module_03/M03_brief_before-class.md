# Before the session: three terms and one connection to try

**Nothing here is graded and nothing is submitted.** There is a short readiness check when the
session opens and it is ungraded too. It exists so I know what to skip.

One thing in here is worth doing early, and it is the last section. It saves you time in the
session rather than costing you time now.

## Three terms

**Term** | **What it means**
---------|------------------
**Second integration** | A second system the platform can reach, alongside version control. Two is a different problem from one, and the next term is why
**Crossing point** | The place where information leaves one system and arrives in the other. Each system reports its own step working. Neither one reports that the handoff did nothing
**Issues in GitHub** | GitHub's own message threads, attached to a repository. Not problems to fix, despite the name. It is what emails you when something lands, and it is how work gets tracked and assigned

The third one is a naming accident and it catches almost everybody. An issue in GitHub is a
thread, like an email chain stuck to your repository. This module you will create five of them
and close them from your commits.

## The one thing worth doing early

Connect **Google Drive** to your platform, the same way you connected version control in module
1. Then check it actually works: put a small file in Drive, ask the platform to read it back,
and see whether what comes back is what you put there.

Then write what happened into your repository, as `module_03/connection-check.md`:

* What you asked for.
* What came back.
* Where it stopped, if it stopped.

A few lines is plenty.

**Why bother, given it is not graded.** Two checks in the lab cover exactly this. If your
connection check is already in your repository and still working when the session starts, those
two are already yours and you start the lab at act 3. If you skip it, you do the same work in
the session and lose nothing. This is a shortcut, not a requirement.

**If it refuses to authorize, you are not stuck and you have not done it wrong.** Institutional
Google accounts vary and this is the first term this has been tried. Write down what the refusal
said and bring that. The lab has a second route that needs nothing from anybody.

## What the readiness check will ask

Three questions, spoken, hands up, thirty seconds.

1. Which of the three terms above did you already know?
2. Did the Drive connection work, refuse, or not get tried?
3. Anything you read here and still could not picture?

Question 3 is the useful one. "I read it and it did not land" is a completely acceptable answer
and it is more helpful to me than a confident nod.

## One warning about the session

You will be handed four files that disagree with each other and asked who deleted something.
Half of that question has an answer. The other half does not, and finding out which half is
which is the work.
