# Technical Journal: third entry

**Where:** your `sec-480` repository, at `journal/module-03.md`
**Due:** Thursday 17 September, 9:00 pm, with the rest of the module.
**Length:** short. A few paragraphs, not an essay.

## What is being graded

Not whether you were right. Whether there is an entry, and whether each answer engages with the
question that was actually asked.

An answer that is two words, or the question pasted back, or an answer to a different question,
gets partial credit. A missing heading gets none. An honest answer saying a thing did not work
and why gets full credit.

That is the whole standard. It is deliberately cheap to satisfy and impossible to fake.

## This module's entry

Eight headings, a few sentences each.

1. **Planning first, and whether it helped.** Act 1. You wrote the tracking before the work
   this time. Did knowing what the five acts produced change how you worked, or did you write
   five issues and then ignore them?

2. **The second system.** Act 2. Did it authorize? What did validating it in both directions
   actually look like, and what would you have missed if you had trusted the settings screen?

3. **The prediction, and what actually happened.** Act 3, first half. Your prediction and the
   real failure. If they matched, say why you saw it coming. If they did not, say what you were
   reasoning from.

4. **The crossing point inside the chain.** Act 3, second half. Where information moved
   between the two systems, and whether anything told you when that step did nothing.

5. **The claim I found hardest to type.** Act 4. One row where `stated` against `inferred` was
   genuinely unclear, and how you decided.

6. **What could not be determined, and how that felt.** Act 4. Writing "cannot determine" as a
   finding rather than as a failure to finish. Was it uncomfortable? Most people find it is.

7. **Where the platform was confident and wrong.** Act 4. It will have been at least once with
   this packet. What was the claim, and what caught it?

8. **The handover I wrote.** Act 5. Somebody is reviewing your work this time rather than you
   reviewing your own. Did that change what you put in the description?

## What a good entry is not

Not a summary of the lab. Not a list of commands. Not a status report saying the work is
complete.

The test is whether someone reading it learns something they could not get from your files.
