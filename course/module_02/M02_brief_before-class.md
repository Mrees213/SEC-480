# Before the session: vocabulary and one small thing to try

**Ungraded.** There is a short readiness check when the session opens. It is also ungraded. It exists so I know what to skip, not so I know who read this.

## What changes from this module on

Module 1 was the one lecture-shaped session of the term. From here the foundational material arrives before the session, and the session itself is working time: labs, coaching, and me circulating rather than presenting.

## Three terms, and what they actually mean

You will meet all three in the session. Definitions here are deliberately short. The session is where they get useful.

**Term** | **What it means**
---------|------------------
**Instruction set** | What the platform reads before every turn, and keeps applying. Distinct from a prompt, which is just the thing you typed once
**Context** | The working set the platform can see right now. It is not memory, things fall out of it, and nothing announces when they do
**Usage window** | A rate limit, daily and weekly. A real operational constraint that will interrupt somebody's work at an inconvenient moment this term

If the first two look like the same thing, that is fair, and it is the distinction the session  opens on. The short version: a prompt is a sentence, and an instruction set is a policy.

## One small thing to try

Take five minutes with any platform you like and ask it to describe a file in your `sec-480`
repository. Any file. Then ask it again in a brand new conversation, without repeating the context you gave it the first time.

Notice what you had to say twice.

That is the entire problem this module solves, and it is much more convincing when you have watched it happen to you than when I say it.

Nothing to submit for this. Just arrive having noticed.

## What the readiness check will ask

Three questions, spoken, hands up, thirty seconds:

1. Which of the three terms above did you already know?
2. What did you have to say twice?
3. Anything on the list you read and still could not picture?

Question 3 is the useful one. "I read it and it did not land" is a completely acceptable answer and it is more helpful to me than a confident nod.

You will be handed somebody else's unfinished work and asked to carry on with it. That is not a metaphor for anything. It is the actual exercise, and it is closer to real work than any lab sheet has been so far.
