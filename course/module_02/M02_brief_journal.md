# Technical Journal: second entry

**Where:** your `sec-480` repository, at `journal/module-02.md`
**Length:** short. A few paragraphs, not an essay.

## What changes this module

The first entry was a log. This one is a reflection, and every entry after it goes further in
that direction.

The reason is simple. A log of the steps you took is reproducible from the lab sheet, so it
tells me nothing I did not already know. Your assessment of those steps is not reproducible from
anything. It only exists if you write it down.

Entries are graded on the quality of the reflection, not on whether the work succeeded. An entry
saying everything went fine is complete and worth very little. An entry saying the platform
reported an analysis with total confidence and got the source of the successful login wrong, and
explaining how that was caught, is what the journal is for.

## This module's entry

Write these five. Headings, a few sentences each.

1. **The process I wrote, and where it came from.** Act 1. What your process does, and
   what somebody else would have to change to use it. If the interview surfaced something about
   how you work that you had not put into words before, that is the interesting part.

2. **Where the platform was confidently wrong.** Act 2. What was the claim, what did it rest on,
   and what made you doubt it? If you never doubted it and only found the problem because the lab
   told you to look, write that down. It is a more useful thing to know about yourself than a tidy
   answer would be.

3. **What my own process missed, and what fixing it cost.** Act 3. The amendment you made, why it
   was not already there, and what you now pay on every future review to have it.

4. **What I could not tell.** Something you finished without being sure it was right. Everyone has
   one. Naming it is the whole exercise.

5. **The handover I received against the one I wrote.** Acts 1 and 4. You were given a note that
   did not do its job, and then you wrote one. What is different about yours, and what did you
   leave out that the next person will need?

## What a good entry is not

Not a summary of the lab sheet. Not a list of commands. Not a status report saying the work is
complete.

The test is whether someone reading it learns something they could not get from your files.
